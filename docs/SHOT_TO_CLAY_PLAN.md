# Shots to clay video: a scene told in cuts (plan, 2026-10-15)

## Goal

Turn a scene cut from many shots, mostly waist-up and closer (a dialogue or a standoff), into a clay reference video for a video model: each shot drawn from its own source camera, the people keeping one place in one room across every cut. Upper bodies come from the video; below the waist each person is a tapered column on the floor, so no leg motion is invented. Test clip: a standoff between six named characters (a Game of Thrones scene, 0–28 s of a 119 s clip kept outside the repo).

## Decisions (user, 2026-10-15)

1. First target: the standoff, 0–28 s (eight shots, before the fight).
2. Lower body: a plain tapered column.
3. Characters are named: Cersei, the Mountain (in full armour), Qyburn, Lancel (carved forehead), a militant with a chain and flail (shot 3), and the other militants as extras.

## Steps

1. **Shots and setups** (built: `authoring/render/shots.py VIDEO START END OUT.json`). Cuts by colour and brightness change against the change around it (a fixed threshold missed cuts in this dark footage); setups by matching the set behind the people (ORB, homography, 40 or more inliers). On the standoff it finds eight shots and joins 2 with 5 (247 inliers) and 6 with 8 (70); by eye 7 is the same side as 2 and 5 but much closer, kept apart. Result in `.authoring/shots/got-standoff-shots.json`.
2. **People per shot** (done 2026-10-15, results below): detect and follow each visible person through the shot (`sam3-track.py` from a point, or the detector where it holds); name them on a sheet; build upper bodies with `reconstruct_motion boxes` (waist-up builds were tested on 2026-10-12: trunk, head and arms near the full-view build, legs invented). A head-only person gets a place and a head turn, no build.
3. **One room** (first version done 2026-10-15, results below): a floor plan with one place per character. Wide shots with several people (1, 2, 4, 5) set the layout; each close shot gets a camera placed so its visible people land where the picture has them, on the floor, at their height; the shot and its reverse face each other across the same two people. Corrected by hand on the Clay page's floor plan (dragging people and cameras).
4. **Draw** (first version done 2026-10-15, results below): a new "shots" scene, the people as plain-shape upper bodies on tapered columns, cut by cut from each shot's camera, on the Clay page and in the export (the `seedance` block names each character by colour).
5. **More of the clip**: the fight (77–92 s), the walk away.

## Risks

- Dark footage and black robes: the detector and tracker lose people; expect hand fixes of who is who.
- The Mountain's armour and helmet: the body model is weakest on hidden body shape.
- Joining shots is the new and uncertain part; with no shared floor in the close shots, places come from layout guesses checked against the wide shots.
- A person who walks in a close shot only slides (no steps).

## Step 3 result, 2026-10-15

- **What each shot says.** For each named person, the pose detector's joints (nose, shoulders, hips, ankles) over the settled last 40% of the shot, or the box for someone seen from behind over a shoulder (`.authoring/shots/got/observations.json`).
- **A solve of everything at once failed.** Places of all characters and all seven cameras (place, height, turn, tilt, lens) fitted to the joints together put the militants on Cersei's side and turned cameras sideways, 50–170 px off. Each shot shows one to four people, too few to fix its camera alone; and size in the picture gives distance divided by the lens, which differs a lot between a wide shot and a close-up.
- **What worked: join the shots' own layouts.** Each shot alone gives its people's places in its camera's frame (sideways from the picture, distance from the nose-to-hip size at known heights) up to its lens. Each layout is fitted onto one shared floor plan by a turn, a shift and a scale (the lens), together, with what is known of how such a scene is shot: the cameras roughly along the line between the two groups (within about 30°), normal dialogue lenses, the militants in front of Cersei's group a few metres off, people in a line about a metre apart. Then each camera's height, tilt and lens are fitted to its picture with the places fixed. Scratch scripts in `.authoring/shots/got/` (`obs.py`, `join.py`, `cams.py`, `overlay.py`); plan in `.authoring/shots/got/plan.json`.
- **Result.** Cersei at the centre, the Mountain 1.4 m behind her and Qyburn beside her; Lancel 2.4 m in front of her, the flail militant at his side, the other militants in rows behind. The cameras of shots 2, 5 and 7 stand behind Lancel facing Cersei's group; 6 and 8 near Cersei facing the militants; shot 4 is a long lens from far behind Cersei's group. One background militant in shot 4 fitted nowhere and was dropped.
- **Checked** by drawing each person's place back onto each shot as a column: in shots 2, 4, 5, 6 and 7 every column stands on its person; in shot 8 (Lancel's close-up) the column is a face-width off; the shot-4 militants are off by a little. The places are a believable reading, not a measurement: the distance between the two groups in particular comes from the assumptions as much as from the pictures. Step 4 needs the Clay page's floor plan to move people and cameras by hand.

## Hand editing on the Clay page, 2026-10-15

## Clay placed from depth, each shot through its own camera (2026-10-18)

The user's question: the hard part of the shots scene is the camera positions; could the quick depth place plain figures instead, since only the clay result is needed? `authoring/render/depth-clay.py DEPTH.npz OUT.mp4 --shots --tracks --people name=colour:height,... [--width] [--video]` does that: no camera solve and no shared room. Every frame is drawn by a pinhole camera at the origin (its lens a guess, 40 degrees wide, which changes only foreshortening, not where figures land), and each named person is a sphere head, torso capsule and column to a level floor at the median of the feet.
- Distance: a person whose head and an ankle are both seen (the pose detector's cached keypoints for the video) is placed from height (lens x height / box height). A person cut by the top or bottom of the picture is placed from box width (0.55 m): in the dark first frames Depth Anything 3 reads the whole frame at one depth, so depth there says nothing. Only a person cut by a side of the picture is placed from depth (scaled per shot by the whole people), never further than their visible width allows. Smoothed over 7 frames.
- What went wrong on the way: a zero-length head capsule gave NaN and hid every figure; a box cut by the bottom counted as a whole body (close-ups came out small and far); box shape alone cannot tell feet in the picture from feet just cut, so the keypoints decide.
- Test: the standoff 0-8.88 s from the saved depth (`.authoring/renders/got-depth-clay/standoff-clay.mp4`, five people, three shots). Each figure lands where its person is and at their size in all three shots, checked on a sheet of 8 frames beside the source. Time: 1.9 minutes at 480 px wide, 7 minutes at 960 (the drawing, a numpy distance-field march, is the slow part), on top of the 3.4 minutes of depth.
- Limits: no room (only the floor), figures do not bend, turn or move their arms, the floor is level (a tilted camera puts it wrong), a person missing from the tracks is missing from the clay, the figures are generic in shape (the Mountain is only taller and wider).
- Shot 3's person had been followed under the name of the flail-bearer; he is Lancel (carved forehead, chain). `depth-clay.py --tracks file.json:old=new` renames a track; rebuilt.

## Clay page: From a video (2026-10-18)

The Clay page has a second section, `/editor/clay.html?video` (header link "From a video"): a clay video made from a source clip, with keyframes. `authoring/render/video-clay-pack.py SPEC.json` writes `.authoring/video-clay/<id>/`: the clay video, a still of the source and of the clay at each keyframe, and `package.json` (people with their colour and who they are, shots with what the camera does, sound, and per keyframe the time, shot, a summary and what each person is doing). The service lists packages at `/api/video-clay` and streams only the files a package names (`/api/video-clay-file`).
- The page plays the clay video with rows for shots, keyframes and sound; a card per keyframe shows source and clay side by side with the description; clicking a keyframe goes to its time.
- "Your scene": who each colour becomes and a style, kept in the browser. From them the page writes a video prompt (clay video as reference video 1, who each figure is, whole-second timeline, camera described at each cut) and an image prompt per keyframe (the clay still for composition, drawn in the style), for regenerating keyframes and matching the clip to another scene.
- First package: `standoff` (0-8.88 s, 8 keyframes at 0.3, 1.23, 2.5, 3.77, 5.04, 6.31, 7.57 and 8.4 s), descriptions written by Claude from the frames. The audio was transcribed only for timing: footsteps until 8.0 s, then the blue figure (Lancel) starts speaking; Cersei answers after this window. What people say and their faces are left for later at the user's choice (the page shows those columns when a package has them).
- Limits: the summaries name the source's roles ("the queen", "the bodyguard"), which a cast name does not replace; the video prompt for 9 s with five people runs to about 2,900 characters; source stills are for checking, not for upload.

## A room behind the figures (2026-10-18)

Tried in order on the standoff, at 480 px: the shot's depth drawn as grey relief (cut-out people left slabs; walls mottled), then plain shapes fitted to the depth (a back wall and columns where something stands in front of it). The user asked for plain shapes recognised in the scene instead of depth, like the stage of the dance scene or the Macbeth fireplace: `depth-clay.py --set SET.json` takes walls, columns and blocks given by eye in picture terms per time span (where each meets the floor, or a distance when no floor is seen), puts them on the shot's level floor and always draws the people in front. `.authoring/video-clay/standoff-set.json` is the standoff's set (16 pieces). The 480 px result reads (floor, back wall with the doorway behind the queen, twin columns, side walls); the 960 px render was stopped at the user's request (about 30 minutes spent), so the Clay page package still has the clay without a room. Limits: the set is guessed by hand and does not follow a moving camera within a span; the floor is level at the people's feet (1.6 m below the camera when no feet are seen).

## Reference video choice on the From a video page (2026-10-18)

There is no Seedance 3.0 from ByteDance (2.5, July 2026, is the latest; sites selling "3.0" are not ByteDance's). 2.5's guide has no depth input, so a depth video can only go in as an ordinary reference video. To compare, a package can carry the tinted depth video of the same window as well (`depth` in the spec; `depth.mp4` and a depth still per keyframe), and the page has a Reference video choice: Clay figures or Tinted depth. The choice changes the video on the page, the second still on each keyframe card and the prompts (for depth: "a depth map ... white is near ... the tints only say who is who; take no colours, greyness or lighting from it", people called "the pink-tinted person"). `depth-tint.py` takes the same `file.json:old=new` track rename; the standoff's tinted depth was remade with shot 3 as Lancel (blue). The comparison itself (one Seedance 2.5 generation with each, same keyframes and prompt) is the user's to run.
